Dog Exercise Guide

Dogs of all sizes need regular exercise to stay physically healthy and
mentally balanced, but the amount and intensity vary significantly based
on their size, breed, age, and energy level. Understanding these
differences helps ensure your dog gets the right kind of activity each
day.

Small Dogs (under 20 pounds) Small dogs, such as Chihuahuas,
Pomeranians, and Dachshunds, often have bursts of energy but tire more
quickly than larger breeds. Despite their size, they still need
consistent daily exercise to prevent obesity and behavioral issues.

Daily duration: 30–60 minutes Exercise types: Short walks, indoor play,
fetch, interactive toys Notes: Many small dogs can meet part of their
exercise needs indoors, especially in apartments. However, outdoor walks
are still important for socialization and stimulation. Be cautious in
extreme weather, as small dogs are more sensitive to temperature
changes.

Medium Dogs (20–50 pounds) Medium-sized dogs like Beagles, Cocker
Spaniels, and Border Collies typically have moderate to high energy
levels. They benefit from a mix of physical and mental activities.

Daily duration: 60–90 minutes Exercise types: Brisk walks, jogging,
hiking, agility training, playtime in a yard or park Notes: Many medium
dogs are working or sporting breeds, meaning they require both physical
exercise and mental challenges. Without enough stimulation, they may
develop destructive behaviors such as chewing or excessive barking.

Large Dogs (over 50 pounds) Large breeds, including Labradors, German
Shepherds, and Golden Retrievers, generally require substantial
exercise, though their needs can vary widely depending on breed. Some
giant breeds (like Great Danes) may need less intense exercise but still
require regular movement.

Daily duration: 90–120 minutes Exercise types: Long walks, running,
swimming, structured play, training sessions Notes: Large dogs benefit
from steady, sustained exercise rather than short bursts. However, avoid
excessive high-impact activity in puppies, as their joints are still
developing. Regular exercise helps maintain healthy weight and supports
joint health.

General Considerations Regardless of size, all dogs benefit from
routine. Splitting exercise into two or three sessions per day is often
more effective than a single long session. Mental stimulation—such as
training, puzzle toys, or scent games—is just as important as physical
activity.

Age and health also play a crucial role. Puppies may need shorter, more
frequent play sessions, while senior dogs often require gentler,
lower-impact exercise. Always adjust based on your dog’s behavior—signs
of adequate exercise include calmness at home and a healthy appetite.

In summary, small dogs typically need 30–60 minutes, medium dogs 60–90
minutes, and large dogs 90–120 minutes of daily exercise. Tailoring
activity to your dog’s specific needs ensures a happier, healthier
companion.
